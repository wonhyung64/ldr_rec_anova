\subsection{KuaiRand Tail-Item Degradation}
\label{app:kuairand_tail}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} shows that, on KuaiRand, every sequential backbone (SASRec,
TiSASRec, FEARec, GRU) obtains \emph{lower} Tail Recall@10 under the proposed framework
than under the backbone alone, in contrast to Micro-Video and MovieLens, where Tail items
improve together with Head and All items. This appendix diagnoses the cause of this
discrepancy directly from the trained models' own outputs, shows that it is resolved by a
single inference-time change requiring no retraining, and identifies what a fully
principled fix would require in future work.

\paragraph{Diagnosing the failure with the models' own outputs.}
At inference the final ranking score is
$s(x_u(t),v) = \eta\log\pi_\Phi(v\mid t) + (1-\eta)f_\Psi(x_u(t),v)$,
with $\eta=0.5$ trained for KuaiRand's sequential backbones (Sec.~4.4). Sweeping $\eta$
post hoc -- without retraining, since $\eta$ is already an inference-time-only parameter
in this framework -- reveals that Tail Recall@10 decreases \emph{monotonically} as
$\eta$ increases, reaching exactly $0$ at $\eta \geq 0.7$ for SASRec, while Head and All
Recall@10 instead \emph{peak} near the trained default $\eta=0.5$
(Table~\ref{tab:kuairand_tail_diagnosis}, left). Isolating the two components shows the
utility-only ranking ($\eta=0$) already exceeds the backbone's own Tail Recall@10 for
every one of the six backbones we tested (Table~\ref{tab:kuairand_eta0_verification}), so
the learned utility term $f_\Psi$ is not implicated; the failure is entirely attributable
to the popularity term $\pi_\Phi$.

Inspecting the learned Hawkes background rate $\mu_v$ directly explains why. For SASRec,
ten items (of 7{,}076) hold 87.3\% of the total $\mu$ mass
(Table~\ref{tab:kuairand_dataset_comparison}; Figure~\ref{fig:kuairand_mu_runaway}b), with
$\max_v \mu_v / \mathrm{median}_v\, \mu_v \approx 5.2\times 10^6$; these same ten items
occupy \emph{every} position in the top-10-by-popularity ranking across 300 randomly
sampled test timestamps, with learned excitation $\alpha_v \approx 0$ -- the ostensibly
``time-varying'' Hawkes popularity has degenerated into a time-\emph{invariant} ranking
dominated by a fixed clique. Because \texttt{tail\_recent\_3d} items are, by construction,
not part of any currently-dominant set, this fixed clique permanently occupies several of
every user's top-10 slots once $\eta$ places non-trivial weight on $\pi_\Phi$,
mechanically suppressing Recall@10 on exactly this evaluation slice. The learned $\mu_v$
tracks each item's raw training-interaction count almost exactly
(Figure~\ref{fig:kuairand_mu_runaway}a: Pearson correlation $0.88$ in log space across the
full catalog; the single item with the largest learned $\mu_v$ is also the item with the
largest raw training count), consistent with $\mu_v$ having no regularization to prevent
this: the ANOVA-centering penalty (Sec.~4.3) constrains only the utility branch
$f_\Psi$, never $\pi_\Phi$.

\paragraph{Why KuaiRand specifically.} The same SASRec+Ours architecture, fit
independently on Micro-Video and MovieLens, shows nowhere near this degree of
degeneracy ($\mu$ max/median ratio $1{,}727$ and $2{,}673$ respectively, versus
$5.2\times 10^6$ on KuaiRand; top-10 $\mu$-mass share $7.1\%$ and $28.3\%$ versus
$87.3\%$; Table~\ref{tab:kuairand_dataset_comparison}). Two natural explanations for this
gap are each contradicted by at least one of the other two datasets: MovieLens has a
\emph{higher} mean interaction count per item than KuaiRand ($132.7$ vs.\ $70.1$) yet a
far smaller $\mu$ runaway, and Micro-Video has a \emph{more} skewed raw popularity
distribution (raw count max/median ratio $404$ vs.\ KuaiRand's $323$) yet the
\emph{least} $\mu$ runaway of the three. What tracks the outcome instead is KuaiRand's
short absolute time span (29.5 days) combined with extreme local density for its most
popular items ($\sim$193 interactions/day for the top item, versus $\sim$15/day on
Micro-Video and $\sim$2.5/day on MovieLens): the resulting median same-item
re-interaction gap is only 21 minutes on KuaiRand, versus 2.0 hours on Micro-Video and
6.8 hours on MovieLens. Because the Hawkes excitation decay rate $\beta$ is a single
scalar shared across all items, it is fit to this typical gap, yielding an excitation
half-life of $0.92$ hours on KuaiRand versus $6.48$ \emph{days} on MovieLens
(Figure~\ref{fig:kuairand_mu_runaway}c). Compounding this, the implementation retains
only the last 50 raw event timestamps per item, \emph{globally} rather than relative to
each query; for KuaiRand's top-$\mu$ item (5{,}501 occurrences over its 28.2-day active
span) those 50 events span only 1.1 days, so $99.1\%$ of the item's own occurrences
precede the retained window and see zero excitation signal by construction
(Figure~\ref{fig:kuairand_mu_runaway}d). MovieLens's much longer (multi-year) span keeps
the analogous window at $684.7$ days, leaving the excitation channel usable across most
of the timeline even though a comparable \emph{fraction} of occurrences (98.0\%) falls
outside it in relative terms. With no regularization on $\mu_v$ and the excitation
channel structurally disabled for KuaiRand's most frequent items, gradient descent has no
alternative but to explain their outsized frequency entirely through an unbounded
$\mu_v$ -- which is exactly what is observed.

\paragraph{Resolution: $\eta=0$ for Tail recommendation.} Since $\eta$ is an
inference-time-only parameter that requires no retraining, we simply set $\eta=0$
(ranking by the utility term $f_\Psi$ alone) when scoring Tail items. Verified over the
same four seeds used throughout Table~\ref{tab:main_results}
(Table~\ref{tab:kuairand_eta0_verification}), this recovers -- and in every case exceeds --
backbone-level Tail Recall@10 across all six backbones (e.g.\ SASRec:
$0.0268\pm0.0013 \to 0.0317\pm0.0014$; TiSASRec: $0.0277\pm0.0012 \to 0.0290\pm0.0017$;
MF: $0.0100\pm0.0007 \to 0.0504\pm0.0008$), fully closing the regression observed in
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} without touching any trained weight. Equivalently, capping
$\mu_v$ at, e.g., its 90th empirical percentile before combining with $f_\Psi$ at the
\emph{original} trained $\eta=0.5$ achieves a similar (if partial) recovery while
retaining some contribution from $\pi_\Phi$ (SASRec: $0.0035\to0.0166$; GRU:
$0.0016\to0.0126$; FEARec: $0.0010\to0.0070$; BSARec: $0.0016\to0.0118$).

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.} The $\eta=0$ fix is sufficient to eliminate the
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} regression without retraining, but it is a symptomatic
rather than a structural correction: it discards the popularity signal for Tail scoring
rather than repairing the mechanism that produced its degeneracy. A principled fix
would need to (i) regularize $\mu_v$ directly -- e.g.\ penalizing $\log \mu_v$ toward its
population mean, or reparameterizing $\pi_\Phi$ as a temperature-scaled softmax over a
bounded item representation, mirroring how $f_\Psi$ is already cosine-normalized -- since
the absence of any such constraint on the popularity branch (unlike the ANOVA-centering
penalty's constraint on $f_\Psi$) is the root cause; (ii) allow the excitation decay rate
to vary across items or a small number of latent timescales, rather than sharing a single
$\beta$ that is systematically misspecified for the minority of items whose burst
dynamics diverge sharply from the dataset-wide median, as they do on KuaiRand; (iii)
make the per-item history cap large enough, or query-relative, that high-frequency items
retain timestamps spanning a meaningful fraction of the dataset's own duration rather
than collapsing to a vanishing window; and (iv) select $\eta$ (and any regularization
strength) against a Head/Tail-\emph{stratified} validation metric rather than an
aggregate one, so that a trade-off of this magnitude is surfaced during model selection
rather than after publication. We leave a full implementation and evaluation of these
changes to future work.
