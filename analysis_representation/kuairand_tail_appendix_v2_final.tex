: utility-only inference already recovers Tail Recall@10 for every backbone, so the

utility model's own Tail discrimination. We now locate its source.

At daily 3-day-window snapshots, KuaiRand's own local popularity is, if anything, less
concentrated and more stable than Micro-Video's or MovieLens's (normalized entropy
$H_{norm}=0.828$, the highest of the three; adjacent-day Jensen--Shannon divergence
$0.039$ bits, the lowest). Yet the trained Hawkes intensity $\pi_\Phi(v\mid t)$ fit to
this data is extremely over-concentrated -- but only on KuaiRand: $H_{norm}$ collapses to
$0.242$ and top-5\% mass rises from $0.520$ to $0.994$, while on Micro-Video and MovieLens
the fitted model is, like the data, mildly \emph{less} concentrated than its own empirical
baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:empirical_vs_hawkes}, Table~\ref{tab:kuairand_concentration_evidence}).
Since this gap does not exist in the data the model was fit to, it is a genuine
model-side over-concentration. Binning items by training-frequency percentile shows why:
$\mu_v$ grows smoothly into the top decile on Micro-Video and MovieLens (37--45$\times$
from the 80--90th to the 99--100th percentile), but jumps discontinuously by
$5{,}872.7\times$ over the same range on KuaiRand -- a qualitatively different,
discontinuous relationship absent from the other two datasets
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism_effect}a).

This over-concentrated term systematically damages Tail ranking once reintroduced via
$s=\eta\log\pi_\Phi(v\mid t)+(1-\eta)f_\Psi(x_u(t),v)$: Tail Recall@10 decreases strictly
monotonically in $\eta$ for all six KuaiRand backbones from $\eta=0.1$ onward, and the
true item's rank worsens for the majority of Tail test events across every backbone
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism_effect}b--c). Because \texttt{tail\_recent\_3d} items are by
construction not part of the currently-dominant set, the small over-concentrated clique
occupies top-10 slots regardless of individual preference, mechanically suppressing
Recall@10 on exactly this evaluation slice.

This vulnerability is not specific to the Hawkes parameterization: replacing it with a
much simpler 3-day sliding-window or static training-frequency popularity -- same utility
checkpoint, same $\eta$ -- damages Tail Recall@10 in the same direction, though less
severely (Table~\ref{tab:popularity_substitution}: utility-only $0.0335\to$ Hawkes
$0.0035$, sliding-window $0.0121$, static $0.0140$). Reintroducing \emph{any} strongly
concentrated, population-level popularity signal through a single global $\eta$ is thus a
general risk for Tail items; KuaiRand's unconstrained, unregularized background-rate
parameterization simply amplifies this risk far beyond what the other two popularity
sources, or the same architecture fit to the other two datasets, produce. The
popularity--utility decomposition itself remains effective -- the utility component alone
already matches or exceeds the backbone on every architecture -- so the fix belongs to the
popularity specification: a bounded/regularized background rate, item-adaptive or
multi-timescale decay, or an explicitly time-varying $\mu_v(t)$ are natural directions for
future work.
