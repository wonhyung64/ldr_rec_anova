\caption{\textbf{Controlled semi-synthetic sweep of the popularity-confounding strength $\rho$.} Synthetic interactions are drawn from $p_\rho(v\mid x_u(t)) \propto \pi_0(v\mid t)^{\rho}\exp\{f^*(x_u(t),v)\}$; $\rho{=}0$ removes popularity confounding entirely, and $\rho{=}1$ (dashed vertical reference line) recovers the paper's own interaction model (Eq.~2). Vanilla and Ours are trained on identical data for every $(\rho,\text{seed})$ pair; markers show the mean over 30 seeds and error bars denote $\pm 1$ SEM. \textbf{(a)} As $\rho$ increases, Vanilla's representation uniformity loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{uni}}$ moves toward zero, indicating increasing concentration / homogenization of user-context representations, while Ours remains essentially flat. \textbf{(b)} The same trend appears in ground-truth preference-ranking recovery: Vanilla's Recall@10 declines sharply with $\rho$, while Ours stays stable. Pairwise Vanilla-vs-Ours differences are statistically significant at every $\rho$ (paired $t$-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank, $n{=}30$; full statistics in Appendix~\ref{app:rho_sweep_stats}).}
